Community Health Score — Methodology

What it measures

The Community Health Score (0–100) answers one question: how well is this community positioned for the household moving here today? It blends where a community stands right now with which direction it is heading, weighted 60/40.

Level Score
60%
Where the community stands today — income, education, schools, safety.
Trajectory Score
40%
Which direction it has been heading since 2010 — income trend, education trend, population, stability.

Level Score components

ComponentWeightHow it's measured
Household Income35%Median household income scaled 0–100 against a $130K ceiling. $65K → 50 pts, $130K+ → 100 pts.
Education25%Share of adults with a bachelor's degree, scaled 0–100 against a 60% ceiling.
School Quality25%Composite school score (0–100) built from US News state rank, Niche grade, ACT/SAT scores, and AP participation.
Crime Safety15%Crime index (70% violent + 30% property) inverted: index 0 → 100 pts, index 40+ → 0 pts.

Trajectory Score components

ComponentWeightHow it's measured
Household Income Trend25%Real % change in median household income 2010→2024, clamped ±30%. –30% → 0 pts, 0% → 50 pts, +30% → 100 pts.
Per Capita Income Trend20%Real % change in per capita income 2010→2024, same scale. Captures high-earner-share shifts that the household median misses.
Education Trend22%Percentage-point change in bachelor's degree share 2010→2024, clamped ±15pp.
Population13%Decline-only penalty. Flat or growing → neutral (50 pts). Decline >5% triggers a ramp down to 0 at –25%. No bonus for growth — built-out suburbs shouldn't be penalized for household-size contraction.
Demographic Stability20%Inverse of racial/ethnic composition churn 2010→2024. Zero churn → 100 pts, 20+ point churn → 0 pts. Rapid composition change is a signal of displacement pressure, not necessarily improvement.

Important caveats

The score weights reflect deliberate judgment calls, not objective truth. Income trend carries the most weight (45% combined between household and per capita) because it's the most direct, legible signal of a community's trajectory — but a user who disagrees with that weight should discount the trajectory score accordingly. Crime data coverage is uneven: Chicago CCAs use a 2022–2024 average from the city's data portal; most Illinois suburbs use 2018–2019 FBI UCR data; some smaller communities have no coverage. Missing components are handled by proportional re-weighting rather than being counted as zero.

All income figures are inflation-adjusted to 2024 dollars. School quality data sourced from US News & World Report (2026-2027 Best High Schools rankings), Niche, and the Illinois State Board of Education's Illinois Report Card (SAT scores, 2024 administration — the last year SAT was Illinois' mandatory state test before the switch to the ACT Suite). Score is recomputed from source data each time the map loads — it is not stored in the dataset.
