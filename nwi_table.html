How to read this table

Each statistic, defined

Per Capita Income

Total aggregate income ÷ Total population

The average income for every resident — including children, retirees, and non-earners. Because it divides by everyone, it runs lower than household measures, but it gives the truest single number for a community's overall economic output per person.

Census Table B19301 · 2024 inflation-adjusted dollars

Median Household Income

The middle value among all households

Half of households in the community earn more than this amount; half earn less. This measure includes the income of every person living together at one address — whether related or not — and is the most commonly cited income statistic in American civic data.

Census Table B19013 · 2024 inflation-adjusted dollars

Median Family Income

The middle value among households of two or more related people

Like median household income, but limited to family households — those with two or more people related by birth, marriage, or adoption. Excludes people living alone or with roommates. Almost always higher than median household income.

This volume covers every Census-designated municipality across the seven Illinois Chicagoland counties (Cook, DuPage, Kane, Kendall, Lake, McHenry, Will) and the three Indiana NWI counties (Lake, Porter, LaPorte) with a population of 4,000 or more, plus all 77 Chicago community areas. Suburb data is pulled directly from the U.S. Census Bureau's American Community Survey (2020–2024 5-Year Estimates). Chicago community area data is sourced from the Chicago Metropolitan Agency for Planning's (CMAP) 2025 Community Data Snapshots, which aggregate the same ACS variables by community area. Median family income is not published by CMAP at the community-area level and appears blank for Chicago entries. Westville (LaPorte County, IN) is excluded from display: the Westville Correctional Facility houses a large incarcerated population that the Census counts as residents, producing per-capita income, age, and education figures not comparable to ordinary civilian communities. Pines (Porter County, IN) is omitted because the Census Bureau suppresses estimates for places of its size due to insufficient sample.
